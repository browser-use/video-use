# Critique rendered motion

## Inspect evidence at the right scale

Review the actual rendered pixels, not only source code. Start with the representative hero frame to catch weak composition before a long render. For the motion pass, capture the first frame, key poses, peak transformation, transitions on both sides of boundaries, and final state. Add frames where motion is fastest or text changes. Uniform contact sheets are useful overviews but can miss a defect between sample times.

Review the complete video at normal playback speed to judge rhythm and continuity. If that is unavailable, use the densest meaningful frame sequence plus audio inspection and state the limitation; stills alone cannot prove fluid motion.

## Two separate reviews

Technical checks establish whether the artifact functions:

- Expected duration, resolution, frame rate, codec, and required audio.
- Assets and fonts loaded, no unintended blank frame, no render exceptions.
- Required copy is complete, readable, and inside its intended frame.
- Seeked frames reproduce the same state, including when requested out of order.
- Labels/data remain correct through intermediate states where applicable.
- Audio has intentional entrances/endings and no unintended clipping or silence.

Artistic critique establishes whether the result serves its direction:

| Dimension | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| Concept | Does the visible transformation belong to the subject? |
| Composition | Does each important pose have clear hierarchy and considered spacing? |
| Typography | Does type feel designed at the delivered size, including optical alignment? |
| Choreography | Do acceleration, arcs, overlap, and settlement express the intended behavior? |
| Rhythm | Do peaks, pauses, cuts, and sound form an intentional progression? |
| Material and finish | Do light, edges, texture, and depth support the visual premise? |
| Coherence | Does the piece remain one visual world while its composition develops? |
| Distinctiveness | Would changing the title make this an unrelated generic promo? |

For a hero-led brief, inspect the sequence with all supporting copy removed. The hero should still carry the composition and progression. Reject invented edge annotations or slogans when they are outside the requested content; after removal, check for a vacant text column and recenter or rescale the subject where needed. In a multi-film review, compare motion mechanisms as well as stills: repeated heading reveals remain repetitive even when their fonts and palettes differ.

Review asset choice and action together. Can the viewer connect the full object to its detail views and transformed states? Does each major movement reveal function, construction, material behavior, a visual relationship or a deliberate change of emphasis? Check attachments, contact and silhouette through intermediate poses. If replacing the hero with any primitive leaves the idea intact, investigate whether the asset is serving the concept. Compare the sourced surface/light proof with its simpler control; more files and more shader effects do not establish an improvement.

Do not convert these into automatic quotas. A still, monochrome, or repetitive piece can be excellent when its premise requires those choices.

## Repair briefs

A useful critique contains time, visible evidence, consequence, and an action. For example: “At 3.8–4.1s the outgoing glyph covers the new word's first letter, so the intended reading is lost; advance the glyph's exit by six frames or shift the new word's anchor.” “Make it more premium” is not a repair brief.

Rank problems by their impact. Repair the concept or weak hero frame before polishing secondary particles. Recheck the smallest affected frame range, then review the full sequence when timing or shared state changes. Stop when the creative contract is met and remaining preferences do not justify more iteration.

For model-assisted critique, provide the actual proof images or video, the creative contract, and relevant reference observations. Ask for a small prioritized set of visible failures. Use an independent critic when it adds perspective. Treat its judgments as hypotheses to inspect, not truth, and avoid self-assigned quality scores as proof of success.

## Comparison and learning

Use identical briefs and delivery specs to compare harness versions. Keep technical failures separate from pairwise preference on concept, composition, motion, finish, and coherence. Save the evidence and revision decisions. A handful of successful demonstrations proves that the workflow runs; consistent blind preference on held-out briefs is stronger evidence of a quality improvement.

Reuse corrections to mechanics and recurring decision failures. Preserve creative freedom: a successful palette, easing curve, or scene structure should become an option, not a universal requirement.
